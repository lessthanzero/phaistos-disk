### 2.1 Sacred Topography
* **Peak Sanctuaries**:
  - *Mount Juktas* (sacred mountain of Knossos): Stepped terrace sanctuaries, stone libation tables inscribed with Linear A libation formulas (`JA-SA-SA-RA-ME`).
  - *Mount Kofinas* (highest peak of the Asterousia range, commanding the southern Mesara plain): Dominates the southern visual horizon of Phaistos; extensive Protopalatial and Neopalatial votive deposits (terracotta figurines, clay anatomical models, bronze blades).
  - *Mount Ida*: Snow-capped massif rising immediately north of Phaistos; hosts the sacred Kamares Cave (source of the palatial Kamares polychrome ware) and the Idaean Cave sanctuary.
* **Sacred Caves**:
  - *Psychro (Dictaean Cave)*: Rich bronze offering hoard, double axes (*labryes*), bronze blades, stepped stone libation tables.
  - *Kamares Cave*: High-altitude sanctuary linked directly to Phaistos; vessel deposits used in libations and feasts.
  - *Amnisos Cave*: Coastal sacred cave dedicated to Eileithyia (childbirth deity, attested in Linear B `e-re-u-ti-ja`).
* **Epiphanic Natural Features**:
  - Natural rock fissures, sacred olive and fig trees, springs, and uncarved sacred stones (*baetyls*). Divine communion was achieved by embracing baetyls or shaking holy trees (*dendrolatry*).

### 2.2 Divine Epiphany
* **Avian Epiphany [DIRECT]**: Minoan deities were not permanently residing in static marble statues. Divine presence was understood as an **epiphany**—a transient descent signaled by sacred raptors, falcons, or doves perching atop double axes or shrines (Ayia Triada Sarcophagus, Knossos Dove Goddess plaques).
* **Ecstatic Vision [STRONG INFERENCE]**: Epiphany was induced through ritual dance, musical accompaniment (lyre, double pipe/aulos Sign 41), and rhythmic invocational chanting.

### 2.3 Votives & Palatial Offering Ledgers
* Physical offerings discovered directly with the Phaistos Disc in **Room 8 of Building 101**:
  - Tablet **PH 1**: Clay offering ledger recording dried figs (`FIC`, Sign 36 parallel) and aromatic cyperus sedge (`CYP`, Sign 37 parallel).
  - **Bovine and Caprine Bones**: Sacrificial offerings of young cattle and sheep, directly matching Signs 26 (`OX HORN`), 27 (`BULL'S LEG`), 28 (`BUCRANIUM`), and 30 (`RAM`).
  - **Kamares Libation Pitchers & Strainers**: Vessels for libations, matching Sign 20 (`HYDRIA HANDLE`) and Sign 43 (`STRAINER`).

### 3.1 Female Divine Power & The "Potnia" Category
* **Rejection of the Universal Monotheistic "Great Goddess"**: Evans and 20th-century mythographers often collapsed all Aegean female representations into a single monolithic "Minoan Mother Goddess." Contemporary scholarship demonstrates a **polytheistic spectrum of specialized, localized female divinities** sharing common iconographic markers.
* **The Structural Title "Potnia" (*Mistress / Sovereign*)**:
  - In Mycenaean Linear B, *po-ti-ni-ja* functions as a primary divine title attached to specific domains, locations, and functions:
    - `da-pu-ri-to-jo po-ti-ni-ja` (*Lady of the Labyrinth*, Knossos KN Gg 702)
    - `si-to-po-ti-ni-ja` (*Mistress of Grain*, Mycenae MY Oi 701)
    - `a-ta-na po-ti-ni-ja` (*Mistress Athana*, Knossos KN V 52)
    - `u-po-jo po-ti-ni-ja` (*Mistress of the Loom/Wool*, Pylos)
* **On the Disc**:
  - Sign 06 (`WOMAN`): Represents the seated or standing Potnia / high priestess in standard Minoan ritual dress.
  - Group B13 (`29-24-24-20-35`): Matches the exact morphological geminate skeleton ($A-B-B-C-D$) of the Minoan Libation Formula dedication `JA-SA-SA-RA-ME` / `A-SA-SA-RA-ME` ("To the Sacred Lady").

### 3.2 Potnia Theron (Mistress of Animals)
* Pervasive glyptic imagery depicts a central female goddess flanked by heraldic animals (agrimia wild goats, lions, griffins, raptors).
* **Comparative Cognate**: Precursor and cultural cognate to the indigenous Cretan goddesses **Britomartis** ("Sweet Maiden") and **Diktynna** (Mount Dikte / hunting net goddess), later absorbed into Classical Artemis.
* **Epistemic Guard**: Avoid equating Minoan Potnia Theron directly with Classical Olympian Artemis; treat Artemis as preserving transformed survivals of this older Aegean complex.

### 3.3 The Bull Complex
* **Horns of Consecration (Sign 26)**: Stone and plaster stylizations of bull horns demarcate the boundaries of consecrated sacred space atop palace parapets, altars, and shrines.
* **Sacrificial Expiation (Signs 27 & 28)**: The bull is the supreme sacrificial victim whose blood is collected in rhyta and poured into subterranean vessels to fertilize the earth.
* **Falsified Retrofit**: The Classical myth of the **Minotaur** (a carnivorous half-bull monster trapped in a maze) is a later Greek Athenian folkloric transformation of Minoan palatial architecture and ritual bull games—**not evidence of a monster cult in MM III Crete**.

### 3.4 The Chthonic Snake Motif
* **Material Parallels**: Faience figures from the Temple Repositories of Knossos (MM III) wielding undulating serpents in each hand.
* **Semantic Field**: Intimacy with the subterranean earth (*chthon*), protection of palatial storage magazines, and symbolic regeneration through the annual sloughing of snake skin.
* **Comparative Cognate**: Cognate to later Mediterranean earth-mysteries (Demeter/Persephone, Eleusis), but rooted in Bronze Age palatial household and tomb protection.

### 3.5 Sacred Mountain / Cave / Epiphany
* Sacred natural landscapes are primary cult locations, not mere landscape scenery.
* **Cretan Zeus (*Zeus Velchanos / Zeus Kretagenes*)**: In later Cretan tradition, Zeus is born in a sacred Cretan cave (Psychro or Ida), nurtured by bees and goats, dies annually, and is reborn with seasonal vegetation. This youthful dying-and-rising vegetation deity preserves the indigenous pre-Greek Minoan male god (*Kouros*, Sign 05).
